**As a** browser player, **I want to** see human characters move and act smoothly in first- and third-person views, **so that** presentation stays aligned with authoritative gameplay.

<objective>
Gap closure for the one `source` verdict of the multiplayer attribution. Plan 07-14 measured the time-limit lifecycle case failing 0 of 3 on current source and passing 3 of 3 before Phase 7. A read-only debug session found no product crash: the test itself races a deadline it sets. It runs a 6-second match, the rematch keeps that setting, and the test's last twenty-odd steps on the second client are still going when the rematched match ends and the game, as designed, releases the pointer and hides the pause menu. Phase 7's first-person views made each step slower under software rendering, which is what tipped a margin that was already under 100 ms.

This is a test fix. It restructures who owns which assertion so that nothing in the time-limit case has to beat its own match limit, keeps every assertion somewhere it cannot race, and adds a case for the behaviour that surprised the old test. It changes no source.
</objective>

## Margin

- Time case after the change: no test step after the rematch is bounded by the 6-second match. The recorder runs inside the page on a 10 ms interval and keeps sampling through generation 2; the test reads the record afterwards, whenever it gets there. The only timing the case asserts is the page's own: the presentation facts and `#mp-resume` must be seen within 1500 ms of the first generation-2 tick, against an expected one-frame lag of 65 to 90 ms, a margin above 15-fold that does not depend on how fast the test process is.
- Score case: the tail measured about 5.3 to 5.9 s on current source against a 180-second match.
- New case: `matchSeconds: 30`. The steps that must finish inside generation 2 are the replacement client's round-trip and the switched client's pointer capture, together about one client's share of the tail measured at 5.3 to 5.9 s for two clients, so roughly 4 s against a 30-second window, above 7-fold. The case records the remaining match time when the switched client's lock is observed and asserts it is above 5000 ms, so a shrinking margin fails as a named margin failure long before it could become a race.
- The part of the time case before the rematch (leave, switch, rejoin during the 15-second results window) is not restructured; it passed in every recorded run. Task 2 measures how much of that window remains when it finishes and reports it.

A bare increase of `matchSeconds` was rejected: it would leave the same race with a different number.
