## Design, decided from measurement

Measured while planning on the real skeleton (forward is +Z, metres and degrees). "Torso line" is the angle from vertical of the line from the pelvis to `spine_03`; "chest pitch" is the forward pitch of `spine_03` itself.

"Lean n" is: `spine_01` rotated forward by n degrees about the character's lateral axis, and `clavicle_l`, `clavicle_r` and `neck_01` each rotated back by the same n degrees. The chest and lower torso lean; both arms and the head keep their orientation and move forward and down with the shoulders. Measured consequence: the trigger hand's orientation is unchanged to the last digit (weapon yaw and pitch 0.0 at every n), and the distance between the two hands stays 0.226 m, so the weapon and the support arm move together.

Why the lean cannot live in `enemyGLTF.js`. The driver takes every spine, neck, clavicle and arm track from the weapon clip (`UPPER_TRACK`, `enemyAnimations.js` line 351), which standing and crouching share; a composed crouch clip contributes only the pelvis and the legs. A pelvis pitch in the crouch clip would pitch the weapon by the same angle (the guard allows 2.5 degrees), and nothing in a clip can add the counter-rotation of the arms for the crouch alone. Changing the driver would reopen the replaced reference-driver baseline of commit `3665406`. The weapon handler in `src/builders/weaponHandling.js` (one of the five `MUTABLE_RUNTIME` files) already runs after mixer, correction and inertia on every actor, rotates arm bones with a saved-and-restored correction per tick, and receives the pose with `crouching`. A lean applied there is restored on the first-person view arms by the existing `rawCorrections` path, so first person is unchanged by construction.

The amount. The owner's word is "slightly". The old armed crouch leaned its torso line 14.4 degrees; 12 degrees of lean gives the composed crouch the same 14.4. That is inside the range of 10 to 20 degrees the orchestrator suggested, so the default is 12 degrees: `export const CROUCH_TORSO_LEAN_RAD = 12 * Math.PI / 180`. It is the only number a second owner pass changes: one line, then the tests of this plan again. The tests read the constant; the fixed guards below bound how far it can go (the look-down limit is the first to bind, at about 20 degrees by the planning figures).

What the lean does not reproduce: the old game also pitched the chest itself 25.9 degrees, standing and crouched, with mocap arms cancelling it. Here the chest pitches 10 degrees in crouch only.

Rule in `updateWeaponHandling`, for an owner that has a skeleton, is not a fixed-anchor (view) owner, and is the owner of the pose's current weapon:
- target is `CROUCH_TORSO_LEAN_RAD` when the pose is alive and `crouching`, otherwise 0;
- the owner's current lean moves toward the target with a time constant of 0.10 s, using the difference of `pose.sampleTimeSeconds` since that owner's previous update, clamped to 0 to 0.1 s; on a reset pose, a non-increasing sample time, or when the owner was not the active one on the previous update, it takes the target at once;
- when the current lean is above 0.0005 rad, the four rotations are applied through the handler's existing save-and-restore (`worldRotate`), before the arm solves, also while a melee, throw, roll or dance suppresses the arm solves;
- when it is not, nothing is done, so a standing actor pays one comparison.

Scope decision on the jump chain: not included. The lean follows the simulated `crouching` state only; the squat of jump start and jump land stays upright. A rule driven by hip height would also lean runners (the run clip drops the pelvis to 0.71 m) and landings, which the owner did not ask for.

Predicted consequences, each measured by the tests of this plan:
- Third person: chest 0.055 m and head 0.091 m further forward than without the lean (head 0.096 m ahead of the root, 1.111 m high; the simulated crouched eye is at 0.85 m); legs, pelvis, feet and every clip-level guard unchanged, because no clip changes.
- First-person arms: unchanged. First-person body view when looking down: the chest is 0.055 m closer to the eye, so the eye is about 0.130 m in front of the chest at level view and 0.230 m at the lower pitch bound (limits 0.10 and 0.60 m unchanged).
- Crouch entry and exit: the lean eases over about 0.3 s; the existing jump of the pelvis position on the first frame (review warning W-03) is not changed by this plan.
